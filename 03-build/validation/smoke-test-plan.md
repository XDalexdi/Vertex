## Objective

**What We're Testing:**
Whether **university students juggling 3+ group projects** want a unified, chaos-free project coordination hub enough to **join a waitlist** for early access.

**Why This Matters:**
Validating that a severe, specific pain point exists in a high-value customer segment is the most critical step before investing in complex software development. If this demand isn't strong, we must pivot before building a single line of code! 💡

---

## Hypothesis

**We believe that** **university students managing 3+ project-based courses** experience **severe coordination chaos and tool-switching fatigue** and **will provide their email** if we promise to solve it with **a single, unified Group Hub solution.**

**We will know we're right when** **12%** or more of exposed users **sign up for the waitlist**.

---

## Test Design

### The Value Proposition

**Problem we're solving:**
Students waste significant time and energy switching between disparate chat, task, and file apps for every single project, leading to missed deadlines and high anxiety.

**Solution promise:**
TeamLearn offers a next-generation "Group Hub" where all communication, task tracking, and file sharing for a project live in **one simple place**, eliminating coordination chaos and giving you complete control.

**Target user:**
University students (undergrad/masters) currently managing 3 or more active, grade-bearing, project-based courses simultaneously.

## Success Criteria

### Primary Metric

**Metric:** Conversion rate (signups / visitors)

**Success thresholds:**
- **Strong validation:** ≥ 20% conversion
- **Good validation:** **12-19%** conversion **(Target!)**
- **Weak signal:** 5-11% conversion
- **Invalid:** < 5% conversion

**Minimum sample size:** **150** visitors

**Rationale for thresholds:**
Our target of 12% is aggressive compared to general web traffic (2-5%) but is justified because we are hyper-targeting a niche (3+ project students) with a *severe* problem. Hitting 12%+ confirms the pain is intense enough to compel immediate action.
