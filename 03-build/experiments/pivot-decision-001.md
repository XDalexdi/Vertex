## Executive Summary

We conducted a smoke test to validate our core customer segment (students with 3+ group projects). The test was highly successful, yielding a 12.5% conversion rate against a 10% target. Our decision is to **ITERATE** by doubling down on this validated customer and immediately proceeding with the prototype validation (Experiment 3) of our core solution, the 'Group Hub.'

### Experiment Results

**Experiment:** 01 (Multi-Project Waitlist Smoke Test)

**Key findings:**
- Finding 1: Conversion Rate was 12.5% (Target: 10%).
- Finding 2: The most highly-converting traffic came from targeted student social channels.
- Finding 3: The primary pain point is "coordination chaos" and tool-switching.

## Rationale for Decision

**Why we're making this decision:**

The success of the smoke test provides high confidence that we have identified a painful, unserved niche: the high-project-load student. The 12.5% conversion rate is a strong signal that building *something* for this group is viable. Therefore, a **PIVOT** (changing customer/problem) is unnecessary, and a pure **PERSEVERE** is too risky without validating the *solution*.

The next logical step is to **ITERATE** by immediately transitioning our focus to the next riskiest assumption: the *specific solution* (S1 - Group Hub). We must confirm that the way we plan to solve their problem—by combining chat, tasks, and files—is actually perceived as better and faster than their current hacky workflow. This means executing Experiment 3 (Prototype Test) and using the qualitative data from Experiment 2 (Interviews) to refine the design before any major code is written.
